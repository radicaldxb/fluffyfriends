# She Didn't Give Birth to Them. But She'd Do Anything for Them.

She rearranges her entire morning around a walk.

She cancels plans when they're sick. She buys the good food — not the cheap stuff. She knows their personality better than most people know their own children. She talks to them. She worries about them. She photographs them constantly and doesn't apologise for it.

She's a dog mum. Or a cat mum. And on Mother's Day, she almost always gets overlooked.

---

## The Problem with "Dog Mum" Gifts

Open any gift guide and you'll find the same things. A mug with a paw print. A tote bag with her breed silhouetted on the side. A cushion that says *"dog mum"* in a font that tries too hard.

These gifts say: *I saw you have a dog and I found something with a dog on it.*

They don't say: *I see how much you love* **her.** *I see that your whole world changed the day you brought her home.*

That's a different kind of gift. And it's rarer than it should be.

---

## Picture This

It's Mother's Day morning.

She comes downstairs. There's something waiting — not flowers that will wilt by Wednesday, not chocolates she'll feel guilty about. Something in a frame, or ready to be framed.

She picks it up. And there's her dog. Not a generic illustration, not a filter on a photo — a real portrait. Painted. Regal. Her dog, rendered as the queen she has always clearly believed herself to be. And there, woven into the artwork itself, is her name.

Not printed underneath. *In* the painting. Part of it.

![A tuxedo cat portrayed as a queen in a personalised oil painting, her name crafted into the crown](/images/misty-queen.jpg)

That moment — that specific, quiet, *oh* moment — is what the right gift feels like.

---

## Why It Lands Differently

Most personalised gifts are personal in a surface-level way. Your name on a necklace. Your initials on a towel. It says *you specifically*, but it doesn't say *this matters*.

A portrait of her pet says something deeper. It says: I know what this animal means to you. I know she's not just a pet. I know you've built your routine around her, your Instagram around her, your heart around her.

And I wanted you to have something that honours that.

![A King portrait of a dachshund hanging on a living room wall, personalised with the pet's name in the artwork](/images/Oscar-portrait.webp)

That's not something you can buy in a petrol station on the way over. That's something someone thought about.

## The Gift She Didn't Know Existed

There's a particular kind of gift that makes someone say *I didn't know this was possible.*

Not because it's expensive. Not because it's complicated. But because nobody had thought to make it — something genuinely personal, genuinely beautiful, genuinely made for her and her pet and nobody else.

![A personalised fireman cat portrait displayed in landscape format above a sofa](/images/Mochi-Landscape.webp)

Dog mums are easy to buy for, actually. You just have to start with the dog.
